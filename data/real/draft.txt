SUBJECT: Executive Summary of Warren Buffett’s 2023 Berkshire Hathaway Shareholder Letter (Dated February 24, 2024)

Executive Overview

In his annual letter dated February 24, 2024, Chairman Warren Buffett pays tribute to the late Charlie Munger, breaks down Berkshire Hathaway’s 2023 financial performance, and outlines realistic growth expectations. Buffett emphasizes that while Berkshire’s massive size precludes "eye-popping" returns, its extreme fiscal conservatism and fortress balance sheet position it to outperform the average American corporation with minimal risk of permanent capital loss.

Tribute to Charlie Munger ("The Architect")

• Passing & Legacy: Charlie Munger passed away on November 28, 2023, just 33 days shy of his 100th birthday. Buffett credits Munger as the true "architect" of modern Berkshire, while describing himself as the "general contractor".
• Strategic Pivot (1965): Having first met in 1959, Munger advised Buffett in 1965 to abandon Ben Graham’s approach of buying "fair businesses at wonderful prices" and instead buy "wonderful businesses at fair prices" to scale Berkshire.

2023 Financial Performance & Key Figures

Buffett reiterates his preference for operating earnings over GAAP net earnings, noting that mandated GAAP figures include volatile unrealized capital gains/losses that can swing by over $5 billion in a single day.
• GAAP Net Earnings (Loss): $96 billion in 2023 (up from a $(23) billion loss in 2022 and $90 billion in 2021).
• Operating Earnings: $37.35 billion ($37.4B) in 2023, up 21.1% from $30.85 billion ($30.9B) in 2022 and $27.6 billion in 2021:
	• Insurance Underwriting: $5,428 million (rebounding from a $(30) million loss in 2022).
	• Insurance Investment Income: $9,567 million (up from $6,484 million in 2022, boosted by higher U.S. Treasury yields).
	• Other Businesses & Misc.: $14,937 million (up from $14,549 million in 2022).
	• Railroad (BNSF): $5,087 million (down from $5,946 million in 2022).
	• Utilities & Energy (BHE): $2,331 million (down sharply from $3,904 million in 2022).
• Net Worth & Scale: Berkshire ended 2023 with the largest GAAP net worth of any U.S. business at $561 billion, representing nearly 6% of the entire S&P 500 universe.
• Historical Returns (1965–2023): Berkshire’s per-share market value rose 15.8% in 2023 (versus 26.3% for the S&P 500). From 1965 to 2023, Berkshire achieved a compounded annual gain of 19.8% (compared to 10.2% for the S&P 500), totaling an overall gain of 4,384,748%.

Core Portfolio & Operating Highlights

• Core U.S. Equities: Berkshire did not buy or sell shares of Coca-Cola or American Express in 2023; each accounts for 4–5% of Berkshire’s net worth. AMEX’s 2023 earnings contribution exceeded Berkshire's original $1.3 billion cost basis, with a ~16% dividend hike expected in 2024.
• Occidental Petroleum: Berkshire increased its stake to 27.8% of common shares at year-end (plus warrants lasting over five years), though Buffett explicitly stated Berkshire has no interest in acquiring or managing the company.
• Japanese Trading Houses: Initiated on July 4, 2019, Berkshire holds roughly 9% stakes in five companies (Itochu, Marubeni, Mitsubishi, Mitsui, and Sumitomo), pledging not to exceed 9.9%. Funded largely via ¥1.3 trillion in yen-denominated bonds, the ¥1.6 trillion investment reached a year-end market value of ¥2.9 trillion, yielding an unrealized dollar gain of $8 billion (61%) plus $1.9 billion in GAAP currency gains (2020–2023).
• Operating Headwinds (BNSF & BHE):
	• BNSF: Faced falling revenues and steep federally mandated wage increases. Over the 14 years since its 2010 acquisition, capital outlays have exceeded depreciation by $22 billion (over $1.5 billion annually).
	• BHE: Suffered severe earnings disappointment due to Western forest-fire liabilities and broken regulatory return pacts in certain states, threatening the traditional utility business model.
• Insurance Strength: Property-casualty operations (led by Ajit Jain since 1986) "carried the day" in 2023, growing volume nearly 5,000-fold over 57 years from $17 million to $83 billion.

Governance & Key Upcoming Dates

• Succession Readiness: Buffett confirmed that Vice Chairman Greg Abel (head of non-insurance operations) is fully prepared to serve as CEO "tomorrow," alongside Ajit Jain leading insurance.
• Annual Shareholder Meeting: Scheduled for May 4, 2024, in Omaha, Nebraska, where the new 4th edition of Poor Charlie’s Almanack will be featured.
